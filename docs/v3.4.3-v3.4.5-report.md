# mimalloc v3.4.3 vs v3.4.5 experiment report

Execution status: Completed for the frozen r23a core scope

Completed at: 2026-08-14T04:52:56Z

Policy: upstream-default

Ratio direction: v3.4.5 / v3.4.3

## Executive summary

The r23a core comparison completed 150 valid records: five workload cells,
two allocator versions, and 15 paired repetitions. There were no invalid or
missing core records. Every measured run lasted between 14.50 and 20.82
seconds.

No core cell showed a resolved throughput difference at the reported
normal-approximation 95% confidence interval for the mean paired ratio; every
interval included 1.0. Median paired throughput ratios ranged from 0.9986 to
1.0092. The two remote-free medians favored v3.4.5 by about 0.9%, but their
variation was much larger than the local cells, so this is not evidence of a
general remote-free improvement.

Peak RSS was similar in most cells. The mixed-wide remote-free cell had very
large run-to-run RSS variation in both versions, including a few low-RSS runs;
its median v3.4.5 RSS was lower, but the paired ratio distribution was too
unstable for a memory-footprint conclusion.

| remote mixed-wide, 4:4 | 467.99 MiB | 445.59 MiB | 0.963 |

The remote mixed-wide RSS samples ranged from about 13–499 MiB for v3.4.3 and
10–509 MiB for v3.4.5. This multimodal/high-variance behavior makes the median
ratio descriptive only. A dedicated memory-retention experiment with RSS time
series and purge-state observation is required before recommending either
patch release for memory reduction.

## Interpretation and recommendations

For these five upstream-default workloads on this host, v3.4.5 is effectively
at parity with v3.4.3 at the resolution of this experiment. There is no
measured performance reason here to reject v3.4.5, but the data also do not
establish a meaningful universal speedup.

For deployments dominated by symmetric remote free, use an application canary
and retain tail-latency, queue-pressure, and RSS monitoring: the synthetic
remote cells had substantially higher variation. For memory-sensitive
mixed-wide remote-free services, run a longer burst/idle or service-specific
RSS-retention experiment rather than relying on the unstable peak-RSS medians.
